# Dashboard

The **Dashboard** is the landing page you see after signing in. It greets you by name and gives you quick access to every module on the platform, plus a rotating set of example circuits and notebooks to explore.

## Welcome header

At the top of the page, a personalized greeting ("Welcome, `<your name>`") reminds you which account you're signed in as, with a short reminder of what the platform is for: learning, building, running, and sharing quantum solutions.

## Quick start cards

Below the greeting, three cards summarize the modules that are available today:

- **Start learning with QCAP** - structured quantum programs delivered as interactive notebooks in a no-setup web environment. Progress from fundamentals to advanced algorithms through hands-on modules, with persistent cloud workspaces and instant feedback.
- **Build circuits with Qompile** - design, simulate, and execute quantum programs through the visual circuit composer. Compose circuits by dragging gates, run them on simulators or real quantum hardware, and export your designs for research or production work.
- **Read & publish on Sqope** - deep-dive tutorials and research from the quantum computing community. Follow creators, bookmark articles, publish your own findings, and keep up with the field.

Each card links directly into its module (`Open QCAP`, `Explore Qompile`, `Open Sqope`) so you can jump straight into the tool you need.

Dashboard and Qanvas appear in the sidebar alongside these modules but don't have their own quick start card yet, since they're still being built out.

## Popular at Qucan

Underneath the quick start cards, a scrollable row highlights a handful of popular algorithms and examples, things like Deutsch-Jozsa, the Quantum Fourier Transform, Quantum Teleportation, Grover's Algorithm, and Bell States, drawn from across the platform. Use the arrows on either side of the row to scroll through more examples.

This list is populated dynamically and is meant to surface commonly used or trending circuits, so the specific examples you see here can change over time. Selecting one opens it directly in the module it belongs to, most commonly Qompile or QCAP, so you can inspect or run it right away.
